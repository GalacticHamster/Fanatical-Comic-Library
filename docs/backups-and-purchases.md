# Recoverable backups and purchase origins (FAN-05 / FAN-06)

**Export backup** saves active Fanatical product identity, bibliographic matching evidence, all purchase origins and account/profile archives. Version 4 saves the wishlist and missing-purchase history, using one queued snapshot so a profile switch cannot mix backup state. Restore accepts versions 1–4, replaces ownership state after confirmation and rejects an invalid later record before writing. Unknown fields, credentials, raw order IDs, redemption values and private download URLs are excluded at every level.

**Export title catalog** is a distinct portable format containing only titles, media types and catalog identity. It excludes purchase dates, bundles, origins, account labels and ownership assertions. A catalog cannot be restored as ownership evidence. Use the full backup for recovery.

Scan fingerprints, active worker leases and session tokens are not recoverable authority. Restoring resets scan history, summaries and failure flags so a new scan rechecks every completed order. Versions 3/4 restore the saved wishlist; versions 1/2 preserve existing wishes. Older backups reset missing-purchase history to empty. Clearing ownership also clears history and leaves wishes intact; a separate control clears them. Pairing/display preferences remain device settings and are not included. Account archives are backup-only and never supply current ownership or Possible candidates.

A successful full scan of the same confirmed profile archives products missing from the new snapshot, preserving their title, safe metadata, purchase origins and first disappearance date. Quick scans and failures never infer absence; initial account assignment does not attribute unknown earlier records to a profile. A returning product leaves history. Profile switches retain the old profile's current and historical evidence in its backup archive and start with empty active history.

Missing active-profile products can warn as **Check ownership**, with the actual earlier title and purchase provenance in the inspector. They never prove Owned, complete package components or collected-content coverage. Missing does not establish that a license was revoked. Current verified ownership takes precedence. History is excluded from catalogue purchase markers and portable title catalogs.

A product is stored once by Fanatical product ID. Each purchase origin retains its bundle/date and an opaque SHA-256 purchase reference; repeated copies of a product in the same order/bundle have a quantity. Raw order IDs never leave the page. Full scans rebuild all observed origins; quick scans merge new origins while preserving older ones and replacing repeated references rather than inflating copies. Legacy backups retain available bundle/date provenance without inventing distinct purchase identities. A full rescan can recover more occurrences from the provider.

The evidence inspector lists origin bundles, dates and quantities, without the private opaque reference. Catalogue ownership recognizes every originating bundle instead of only the most recent one. Tests exercise separate purchases on full/quick scans, duplicate quantities, idempotence, secrets in nested origins and profile-archive backup round trips.
