# Recoverable backups and purchase origins (FAN-05 / FAN-06)

**Export backup** saves active Fanatical product identity, bibliographic matching evidence, all purchase origins and account/profile archives. Version 4 saves the wishlist and missing-purchase history, using one queued snapshot so a profile switch cannot mix backup state. Restore accepts versions 1–4, replaces ownership state after confirmation and rejects an invalid later record before writing. Unknown fields, credentials, raw order IDs, redemption values and private download URLs are excluded at every level.

**Export title catalog** is a distinct portable format containing only titles, media types and catalog identity. It excludes purchase dates, bundles, origins, account labels and ownership assertions. A catalog cannot be restored as ownership evidence. Use the full backup for recovery.

Scan fingerprints, active worker leases and session tokens are not recoverable authority. Restoring resets scan history, summaries and failure flags so a new scan rechecks every completed order. Versions 3/4 restore the saved wishlist; versions 1/2 preserve existing wishes. Older backups reset missing-purchase history to empty. Clearing ownership also clears history and leaves wishes intact; a separate control clears them. Pairing/display preferences remain device settings and are not included. Account archives are backup-only and never supply current ownership or Possible candidates.

A complete full scan of the same confirmed profile archives products missing from the new snapshot, preserving their title, safe metadata, purchase origins and first disappearance date. Quick scans, partial scans and unsafe failures never infer absence; initial account assignment does not attribute unknown earlier records to a profile. A returning product leaves history after a complete scan. Profile switches retain the old profile's current and historical evidence in its backup archive and start with empty active history.

Missing active-profile products can warn as **Check ownership**, with the actual earlier title and purchase provenance in the inspector. They never prove Owned, complete package components or collected-content coverage. Missing does not establish that a license was revoked. Current verified ownership takes precedence. History is excluded from catalogue purchase markers and portable title catalogs.

A product is stored once by Fanatical product ID. Each purchase origin retains its bundle/date and an opaque SHA-256 purchase reference; repeated copies of a product in the same order/bundle have a quantity. Raw order IDs never leave the page. Full scans rebuild all observed origins; quick scans merge new origins while preserving older ones and replacing repeated references rather than inflating copies. Legacy backups retain available bundle/date provenance without inventing distinct purchase identities. A full rescan can recover more occurrences from the provider.

The evidence inspector lists origin bundles, dates and quantities, without the private opaque reference. Catalogue ownership recognizes every originating bundle instead of only the most recent one. Tests exercise separate purchases on full/quick scans, duplicate quantities, idempotence, secrets in nested origins and profile-archive backup round trips.

## Reporting an incomplete purchase scan

If an import fails, your previous purchases and successful scan history are kept. On Fanatical's Orders page, open **Scan diagnostics** below the import result and choose **Export scan report** before leaving or reloading the page. The report contains extension/browser information, requested and selected scan modes, failure category, stage and aggregate counts, including the last acquisition phase, checked/active/failed detail operations and elapsed time. It does not contain titles, order numbers, account details, page HTML, credentials or payment information. Nothing is uploaded automatically.

During import, progress distinguishes purchase-page discovery, purchase-detail checks, validation and saving. One purchase may contain many products; the detail counter counts purchases, not every product. Elapsed waiting feedback means a response is still pending, not that the import has failed or finished. Unreadable detail checks advance the checked counter and remain eligible for the existing recovery workflow. Progress is transient and adds no per-purchase storage writes. The final result appears only after the worker confirms the save. Leaving Orders detaches its progress panel; the existing worker-owned import may continue, so return to Orders to see its result.

Send that report with the error message when asking for help. Do not send a full library backup unless separately requested: a backup contains purchase information and is different from this diagnostic report. A report can show that a session was accepted or a local account was selected; neither proves which website account is signed in.

An incomplete scan is not a successful complete-library baseline. A **partial import** can still save independently verified purchases: one unreadable order does not discard other verified orders, and a missing page does not remove old purchases. The result states how many purchases were verified and how many new products were added. A purchase containing an unreadable product is left unresolved as a whole, rather than saving only part of that purchase.

Partial imports merge records in one save, retain manual corrections and missing-purchase history, and leave the last complete-scan timestamp unchanged. Only verified orders gain skip eligibility. Unresolved orders lose skip eligibility and are retried on a later scan. Quick still reads the listing; if a valid partial checkpoint exists it can skip verified details while retrying the unresolved ones. If no valid initialization or partial checkpoint exists, Quick initializes through Full. A later complete Full can perform normal reconciliation. When every returned order is readable and the request finishes normally, the result says **Import finished** with verified-purchase and added-product counts. Coverage qualifications appear in the expandable **Scan diagnostics** details and explicit report export, not as a warning in the normal success status. If Fanatical supplied no total or continuation information, full-history coverage remains unverified: existing purchases are retained, and no complete-scan timestamp advances. Quick can still skip independently verified orders and look for new purchases. Running Full again cannot prove coverage if the provider supplies the same ambiguous response; it rechecks the returned purchases. Actual interrupted pagination or unreadable orders continue to report partial acquisition and retry information.

Session changes, conflicting purchase identity, unsafe account selection and failed storage commits save no purchases from that operation. Retry after signing in if the message identifies a session problem, or retry later for transient request failures. Do not clear the library or assume that installing the large-library storage-permission update will repair provider pagination or product structure.

When saved order evidence exists, the current listing must share exact purchase identity with that saved account before a partial merge is allowed. A completely disjoint listing can mean the wrong website account; it can also mean pagination stopped before any older purchases were reached. The extension refuses that ambiguous save rather than mixing accounts. Older nonempty libraries with no order identity need the existing **Advanced accounts** confirmation before acquisition. New empty accounts do not need that extra step. Matching nicknames alone never establish identity.

The scan report separately identifies whether acquisition finished and whether complete coverage was verified. It includes outcome, verified purchases saved, unresolved count, page progress, response-shape counts, readable-header counts, presence counts for total/range/link headers and recognized next references, discovery evidence and checkpoint advancement. It never exports header values, bodies, identifiers or URLs. Partial checkpoint and retry metadata stay local and are reset on restore; the verified purchases themselves remain in the normal backup. Authorized GL synchronization can share these saved purchases through the existing partial, historical, additive contract. It does not claim a freshly complete website scan, and an absent or unavailable companion does not block the local save.

Existing companion transfer limits still apply. For example, an unusually large single product record with hundreds of purchase origins can exceed the per-record transfer limit. Purchases remain saved in FAN, while sharing reports Needs attention instead of sending an incomplete record. This limit is separate from browser storage quota and does not justify clearing purchases.
